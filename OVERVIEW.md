# PackAR — Project Overview

An iPhone app that measures your suitcase and the things going into it with the depth camera, solves the 3D packing problem on device, and guides the pack step by step with an AR overlay inside the actual bag.

---

## The idea in one paragraph

Point the phone at your open suitcase and at your stuff. The app captures the geometry of both, computes an arrangement that maximizes what fits under real constraints (airline weight limit, fragility, balance, what you need to reach at security), and then walks you through placing each item — current item rendered solid in position, already-placed items dimmed, future items hidden.

## Why it's not trivial

Three constraints drive every design decision:

1. **Depth sensing is coarse.** The iPhone depth sensor is roughly 256×192 with centimeter-scale noise — built for room-scale AR, not for measuring a dopp kit to a couple of millimeters. Mitigated with multi-frame TSDF fusion, plane fitting, a catalog of pre-scanned bags, and a 3–5% inflation on every measured dimension. A plan that's slightly roomy feels magical; a plan that's 4mm tight feels broken, permanently.

2. **Most contents aren't rigid.** Clothes are roughly 70% of suitcase volume and are compressible, foldable, stuffable. Classic bin packing assumes rigid boxes. Solved by giving each item a rigidity class and compressibility factor `k`, then treating soft items as a final void-filling pass: an item of loose volume V fits any connected void of at least V/k.

3. **Suitcase interiors aren't boxes.** Wheel wells intrude into corners, the telescoping handle leaves raised rails along the back panel, the lid has its own shallow compartment. Modeling the container as a rectangular prism throws away 10–15% of usable volume. The container is a 1cm voxel occupancy grid with named zones instead.

## Architecture

```
Capture  ->  Geometry  ->  Solver  ->  Guidance
```

| Stage | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | Depth frames, RGB, catalog lookup, barcode scan | Fused bag mesh; per-item masks and depth patches |
| Geometry | Meshes and masks | Voxel grid for the container; OBB plus attributes per item |
| Solver | Container voxels, item set, constraints | Ordered placement list: item, position, orientation, zone |
| Guidance | Placement list, live camera pose | Step-by-step AR overlay plus a 2D layer diagram |

### Capture

**Bag** — three tiers, ship all three:
- *Catalog lookup (best):* pick make and model, serve a pre-built interior mesh with wheel wells and handle rails already modeled. Seeded by scanning the top ~200 bags. Nobody else has a bag-geometry database — this is the durable asset.
- *Guided scan:* bag open and empty, lid flat, slow orbit; fuse depth frames into a TSDF, then plane-fit walls and floor.
- *Manual fallback:* tap the four inner corners and the top rim. Ten seconds, surprisingly accurate.

**Items** — don't build meshes. For ~95% of objects an oriented bounding box plus a class label is all the solver needs: lay items on a table, one capture, segment each object, lift the mask against the depth map, fit a minimum-volume OBB. Reserve full photogrammetry for irregular high-value items. Barcode lookup handles packaged goods with no scanning at all.

### Geometry model

This is where the real IP sits — the solver is a known algorithm, the representation is not.

```
Item {
    obb               : (l, w, h) oriented bounding box
    mass              : kg
    rigidity          : rigid | semi | soft
    k                 : compressibility factor (soft items only)
    fragility         : 0..1
    orientation_lock  : none | this_side_up | flat_only | horizontal
    access_priority   : 0..1   security bin, first night, etc.
    nestable_volume   : interior capacity, if any
}
```

Four fields carry most of the value:
- **k** makes clothes tractable without simulating cloth.
- **orientation_lock** — liquids are this-side-up, laptops flat-only, bottles horizontal and padded.
- **access_priority** — laptop and liquids come out at security; night one's outfit isn't at the bottom.
- **nestable_volume** — shoes, mugs and pots have interiors. Socks go in shoes. Recursive packing, and the highest-leverage trick expert human packers use.

### Solver

3D container loading with stability and orientation constraints — NP-hard. No exact MIP; it won't return in three seconds on a phone.

| Pass | Method |
|---|---|
| Nest | Stuff small items into `nestable_volume` containers (runs first — changes the item set) |
| Order | Priority key: volume × density × rigidity, generated by the metaheuristic |
| Place | Empty Maximal Space / Extreme Point with deepest-bottom-left-fill |
| Search | Biased random-key genetic algorithm or simulated annealing over orderings and orientations |
| Fill | Flood-fill remaining connected voids, assign soft items with compression (last — soft items are the only ones that adapt) |

```
maximize    packed_value
subject to  total_weight <= airline_limit
penalize    | CoM - ideal_CoM |                    balance
penalize    fragile items with no soft neighbor
penalize    high access_priority items buried deep
penalize    items with < 70% base contact          stability
```

The center-of-mass term is the non-obvious one: when the bag is upright and rolling, *down* is the wheel end, not the shell floor you packed against. Heavy items belong at the *end* of the bag as it lies flat open — the opposite of what the packing view makes you want to do.

**Runtime:** on device. Rust or C++ core behind a Swift FFI, voxel ops in Metal if needed. Target under 3s for 30 items.

**The requirement people forget:** the solver must re-solve from a partial state. Humans will deviate at step four. It has to accept "here's what's actually in the bag now" and re-plan the remainder. Without that it's a one-shot demo, not a tool.

### Guidance

- **Anchoring:** plane detection to find the open shell, then ICP against the scanned interior mesh; persist a world map. Fallback: tap two inner corners.
- **Rendering:** RealityKit, translucent labeled prisms, scene-depth occlusion so real objects hide virtual ones. Without occlusion it looks like a sticker over a camera feed.
- **Step mode:** current item solid with a placement arrow, placed items at 15% opacity, future items hidden. Thirty overlapping ghost boxes are unreadable.
- **Ship the 2D layer view too, and expect it to be what people use.** AR means holding a phone while both hands are packing. A layer-by-layer exploded diagram is more practical. AR is what makes people download it; the layer cards are what makes it useful.

## Stack

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Capture | ARKit — scene depth, scene reconstruction, Object Capture |
| Segmentation | Vision foreground instance masks, or a mobile SAM variant |
| Render | RealityKit with scene-depth occlusion |
| Solver | Rust or C++ behind Swift FFI; Metal for voxel ops |
| Local data | SQLite or Core Data for the item library |
| Sync | CloudKit |
| Server | Bag-model catalog, airline size and weight rules |

## Build order

| Version | Scope | Proves |
|---|---|---|
| v0 (~2 weeks) | No AR, no LiDAR. Manual dims or catalog pick, items typed or roughly photographed. Solver plus 2D layer diagram. | Whether the algorithm beats a human. Everything else is downstream. |
| v1 | Depth bag scanning, photo-based item capture, persistent item library | That capture is fast enough people will actually do it |
| v2 | AR step mode, depth occlusion, world-map persistence | That the overlay is legible and trustworthy in a real room |
| v3 | Re-solve from partial state, weight estimation, airline rule checking, packing-list generation | Retention — the item library is the loop |

## The gate

Before writing app code: pack ten real suitcases by hand and measure volume utilization. Pack the same items following the algorithm's plan and measure again. Humans typically land around 60–70%. If the solver can't clearly beat that, no amount of AR polish saves the product. One weekend, highest-information thing available.

## Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Infeasible plans | Fatal | Dimension inflation, tolerance margins, visible confidence indicator |
| Capture friction | High | Catalog lookup, barcode scan, scan-an-object-once item library |
| Deformables | High | Coarse compressibility model, improved with usage data (itself a moat) |
| Low use frequency | High | Item library, packing lists, vertical markets |
| AR ergonomics | Medium | 2D layer view ships at the same time as the hedge |

## Where this actually makes money

Honest read: consumer travel packing is a low-frequency, mild-annoyance problem. Great demo, hard retention story. The same engine is worth real money where packing is frequent, high-stakes, or billed:

- **Case and foam layout** — hard cases for camera crews, audio engineers, drone operators. The output isn't AR, it's a CNC-cuttable foam insert plan. People pay hundreds for this today and it's done by hand.
- **Field service and medical kit bags** — restocking a standardized kit, verifying nothing's missing, fitting a fixed loadout into a fixed case.
- **Carton selection in fulfillment** — pick the smallest box that fits the order. Directly reduces dimensional-weight shipping cost, a line item that already has a budget.
- **Movers, van builds, race-team logistics, trade-show kits.**

Build v0 with the consumer framing because it's fastest to test, and keep the geometry model general enough that a foam-lined hard case and a 23kg checked bag are the same object with different constraints.
